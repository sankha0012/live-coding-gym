Expected shares are [0.3, 0.3, 0.3, 0.1] and actual shares are [0.0, 0.0, 0.3, 0.7]. The clip replaces the zero shares by 0.0001, so the logarithm of a ratio stays finite. PSI is the sum over bins of (actual - expected) times log(actual / expected). The two emptied bins contribute about 2.4 each, the third contributes 0, and the last about 1.17, so psi prints 5.97, which is above the 0.25 cutoff, so the drift is "high".

Answers. The share lists are counts per bin divided by the sample size. If the data has an empty bin and the code does not clip, log(0) or a division by zero gives -inf, inf or nan, and the PSI is useless or nan, which then fails every comparison silently; clipping to a small epsilon is the standard fix. A PSI of 5.97 means the score distribution moved strongly toward the top; the common rule of thumb is that above 0.25 is a major shift. The next step is to find the cause (an upstream feature, a population change, a bug) before retraining, and to use the same bin edges, taken from the reference, on every run.
