scores holds five model outputs and the threshold is 0.5. The first list comprehension keeps every score that is at least 0.5, giving [0.92, 1.3, 0.51], and the rate is 3 of 5, which prints 0.6. The mean of all five is (0.92 + 1.3 + 0.48 - 0.2 + 0.51) / 5 = 3.01 / 5, printed as 0.602. The clean list keeps only scores between 0 and 1 inclusive, so 1.3 and -0.2 are dropped: kept 3, dropped 2. The mean of 0.92, 0.48 and 0.51 is 0.637.

Answers. 0.92 and 0.51 pass legitimately, but 1.3 also passes the threshold and is not a probability at all. The check only asks "is it at least 0.5", never "is it a valid score". If the data contains 1.3 or -0.2 (a bad sigmoid, a raw logit, a unit bug, a different model version), the flag rate and the mean are computed on garbage, and nothing crashes. Dropping the two invalid values moves the mean from 0.602 to 0.637, and it hides the real problem unless you also count and alert on the dropped rows. The defence is to validate the range at the boundary, then either reject the batch, clip with a logged warning, or fall back, depending on what the product can tolerate.
