Ninety-nine percent accuracy does not tell me much here, because a model that says "not fraud" for every transaction also scores 99% and catches no fraud at all. Accuracy mixes both classes, and the majority class dominates it. I would look at precision and recall for the fraud class. Precision tells me what fraction of the alerts are real fraud, and recall tells me what fraction of the real fraud we catch. F1 combines them as a harmonic mean, so a model that is strong on only one of them gets a low score. For the always-no model, recall is zero. Then I would connect the metrics to money: a missed fraud costs the transaction amount and trust, a false alert costs analyst time and a blocked customer. That decides the threshold and the target, for example high recall with a precision the review team can handle. I would also report the counts behind the metrics, because with 1% positives a small test set can have very few frauds and the numbers are noisy.
