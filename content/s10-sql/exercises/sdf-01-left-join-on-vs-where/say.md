They do not mean the same thing once the join is a LEFT JOIN. The ON clause decides which orders match a customer, and a customer with no matching row is still kept, with NULLs in the order columns. WHERE runs after the join and filters the joined rows. If I write `WHERE o.status = 'delivered'`, a customer with no orders has a NULL status, the comparison is not true, and that row is removed. So the customer who should show 0 disappears, and the LEFT JOIN has behaved like an inner join. A customer with only cancelled orders also disappears for the same reason. Because the report must list customers with zero delivered orders, the delivered condition describes which orders to match, so it goes in ON, and I count `o.order_id` rather than `*` so a customer with no match gets 0 and not 1. When I want the opposite question, customers who have no order, the NULL test goes in WHERE after the join, because there I do want to filter after matching. To check, I compare the number of customers in the result with `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM customers WHERE city = 'Curitiba'`, and they must be equal.
