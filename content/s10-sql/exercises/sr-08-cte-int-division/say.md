The query is a chain of two common table expressions. per_city groups orders by city and computes two integers: n_orders with COUNT(*) and n_cancelled with a SUM over a CASE that is 1 for cancelled rows and 0 otherwise. That gives Natal 4 and 3, Recife 3 and 1, Salvador 1 and 0. The second CTE, rates, divides n_cancelled by n_orders to get cancel_rate. The final SELECT keeps the cities whose rate is at least 0.3, ordered by city. The intent is to list the cities with a high cancellation rate, which should be Natal (0.75) and Recife (about 0.33).

I ran it and it returns zero rows. In SQLite, dividing an integer by an integer gives an integer, truncated toward zero: 1/3 is 0, 3/4 is 0 and 0/1 is 0. Every cancel_rate is 0, and 0 >= 0.3 is false for every city.

The trap is silent integer division. It does not raise an error, it just produces a wrong, plausible-looking number. The fix is to force a real number, for example n_cancelled * 1.0 / n_orders or CAST(n_cancelled AS REAL) / n_orders. I ran that version and got Natal 0.75, Recife 0.3333333333333333 and Salvador 0.0, so the filter would keep Natal and Recife. Other engines behave differently (PostgreSQL also uses integer division here, some others do not), so know your dialect.
