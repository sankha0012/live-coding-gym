Cron only starts a command at a given time. An orchestrator such as Airflow models the work as a graph of tasks with dependencies, so I can say that training runs only after ingestion and validation succeed. It also gives retries, alerts when a task fails, a record of every run, and the ability to rerun a past day, which is called a backfill. With cron, if the ingest job fails at night, the training job still starts on stale data and nobody is told. For a single, independent script, cron is enough, and an orchestrator adds setup and something else to operate. I would move to an orchestrator when several steps depend on each other and failures must be visible.
