Answers. The first run makes 3 calls and the second makes 1: the same failing request was repeated three times although nothing could change between attempts. In production, retrying a client error such as a 400 or a validation failure wastes capacity and time, adds load to a service that may already be struggling, delays the real error by the sum of the backoff sleeps, and multiplies the damage if the call has side effects. Retry only transient failures, where trying again can plausibly succeed: timeouts, connection resets, 429 and 503 responses. And only when the operation is idempotent, or carries an idempotency key, so a retry after an ambiguous failure cannot do the thing twice.

Trap: except Exception in a retry loop. Defence: retry an explicit allow-list of transient errors, cap attempts, add backoff with jitter, and let everything else fail fast.
