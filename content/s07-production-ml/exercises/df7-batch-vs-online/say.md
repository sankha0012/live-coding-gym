I decide by asking when the input is known and how fresh the answer has to be. The morning email uses data from yesterday and is read once a day, so a nightly batch job fits. It is cheaper, simple to rerun if it fails, easy to backfill, and nobody waits for it. The checkout warning depends on the current cart and the courier supply right now, so a nightly score would be stale and cannot see the cart. That one needs online serving, where I accept the extra cost of a service that stays up, with a latency budget, a timeout and a fallback such as showing no warning, because a slow scorer must never block checkout. What I give up with batch is freshness, and with online it is simplicity and operational load. A batch failure is quiet: if the job dies, the email still goes out with yesterday's scores, so I would check that the output exists, is fresh and has the expected row count. Online fails loudly but under load, so I would watch latency percentiles and the fallback rate. If both paths need the same feature logic I would share it to avoid training and serving skew.
