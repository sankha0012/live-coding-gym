I would use all three in order, because they answer different questions. First a shadow deployment: the new model receives a copy of real requests and its answers are logged, not shown. This tells me about latency, errors, the new feature being available, and how much its predictions differ from the old model, with no risk to customers. Then a canary: a small share, for example 5 percent, gets the new model for real. I assign users by hashing a stable id with a salt, so a user always sees the same arm and raising the percentage only adds users. The canary tells me about health under real traffic, and I set the rollback condition before the start, such as p99 latency above the budget, a rising fallback rate or an error spike. Because the model is slower, latency is the likeliest reason to roll back. If I must prove that the better accuracy improves a business metric, such as fewer late-delivery complaints, I run a proper A/B test with enough users and time, and I do not read a canary as an A/B result. Shadow cannot show how customers react, and an A/B test is slower, so I would not skip the earlier stages.
