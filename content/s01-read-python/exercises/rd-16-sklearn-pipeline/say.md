The script has six rows with two features each, the second feature on a much larger scale, and labels 0 for the first three rows and 1 for the last three. It builds a Pipeline of two named steps: a StandardScaler and a LogisticRegression with random_state=0. pipe.fit learns the scaler's mean and standard deviation on X, transforms X, and fits the classifier on the scaled result. predict applies the already fitted scaler to the new rows and then calls the classifier. It prints the two predictions, the accuracy on X, the scaler's learned means, and the step names.

Answers. fit calls fit_transform on every step but the last and fit on the last; predict calls only transform on the scaler, never re-fitting it, which is what keeps the new data from changing the statistics. The score is 1.0 because the model is evaluated on the same six rows it trained on and the classes are cleanly separated; that says nothing about unseen data. mean_ is [3.5, 210.0]: the column means of the training data, printed as "[  3.5 210. ]" because numpy pads the array text.

The trap. Reading the score as quality: it is a training score on six points. The predictions [0 1] are right for these easy rows only because the groups are far apart. random_state=0 is there for reproducibility, even though this solver is effectively deterministic here.
