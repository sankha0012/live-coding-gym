Worked sample, shaped like a real answer: "Our support team was slow to answer repeat questions, and agents lost time searching old tickets. It mattered because response time was the main number the team was judged on. I owned the design and the prototype of an assistant that suggested answers, and an engineer helped me move it into the existing tool. The main decision was to let the assistant suggest answers that the agent approves, instead of sending answers automatically, because a wrong answer to a customer is costly and we had no way to measure quality yet. Average response time dropped by about a quarter in the first month, and agents rated the suggestions in the tool, which gave us data to improve. Next time I would build the rating and a small test set before the launch, not after."

Why it works: one problem, one decision, one number, one lesson. It names a trade-off (automatic versus approved) and shows judgement. Keep your own numbers honest. If you do not know an exact figure, say "roughly" and give the range.
